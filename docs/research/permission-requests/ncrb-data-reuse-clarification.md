# Draft request: NCRB, reuse of district-level tables

**Status:** UNSENT DRAFT, prepared 10 November 2026. Do not send until the project owner explicitly approves it.

**Background:**
- **OR-12:** no explicit NCRB reuse licence has been found. The NCRB copyright page could not be opened because of a certificate error.
- **data.gov.in:** says its content is licensed under the Government Open Data License – India. However, no 2020–2022 Delhi district crimes-against-women resource was found there.
- **SafeHer's holdings:** official NCRB district files for 2014–2016 and 2020–2022 ([district assessment](../ncrb-district-data-assessment.md)). The 2020–2022 files reconcile exactly with the Delhi UT totals.
- **Map status:** the district map is blocked partly by this issue ([district map recommendations](../district-map-production-recommendations.md)).

**Update (10 November 2026, completion round):** NCRB's copyright statement has now been read: <https://ncrb.gov.in/copyright-statement>, available in Hindi only.
- **What it says:** material should not be copied or reproduced, in part or in full, without the Bureau's prior permission. Downloading is allowed. Use in a misleading or objectionable context is barred, and any other use is subject to permission.
- **What this changes:** the request below now asks for permission, not only clarification. It names every SafeHer display affected:
  - the Delhi-wide Table 3A.1 totals on Crime Trends, Overview and Data & Sources;
  - the 2020–2022 police-district figures on the Crime Map;
  - the 2022 district categories.

  See the [publication-rights matrix](../safeher-publication-rights-matrix.md).
- **Contact:** the copyright page names `dct[at]ncrb[dot]gov[dot]in`, which is the Data Centre & Technical branch. A publications or statistics contact may be more appropriate.

**Subject:** Request for permission to reproduce NCRB figures on a non-commercial academic website (SafeHer)

To: [Office to be confirmed: Statistics Branch / publications contact], National Crime Records Bureau, New Delhi

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am [name], a [programme] student at [institution], supervised by [supervisor name]. SafeHer is a non-commercial academic website that presents NCRB's published statistics on crimes against women in Delhi.

SafeHer:
- cites each figure to its table and edition;
- keeps NCRB's original category labels;
- separates non-geographic police units;
- does not rank areas or present safety scores.

NCRB's copyright statement asks for the Bureau's prior permission before its material is reproduced. I am therefore requesting permission for the uses below, and would be grateful for clarification of any conditions. The figures concerned are:
- Delhi UT annual totals of crime against women, 2016–2024, from *Crime in India* Table 3A.1. They are shown as a trend chart and a table.
- Delhi police-district figures from the *District-wise Crime against Women* spreadsheets, 2020–2022:
  - the total crime against women for 16 districts and 7 non-geographic units;
  - 20 headline categories for 2022.

  They are shown as a table and, if boundaries can be licensed, as a shaded district map.

1. **Reuse rights.** May these figures be reproduced on a non-commercial website? Does the Government Open Data License – India apply to them?
2. **Reproduction and visualisation.** Is permission given to reproduce selected figures and present them as charts and tables?
3. **Derived geographic visualisations.** May police-district figures be shown as a map that joins NCRB counts to police-district boundaries from another source? The map would carry a statement that the boundaries are not NCRB's.
4. **Attribution.** What attribution or citation wording does NCRB require?
5. **Definitions and tables.** Must the original category definitions, table numbers or notes accompany the data? SafeHer currently links each figure to its source table and shows NCRB's caveats.
6. *(Optional, technical.)* Are district-wise reports for Delhi available for 2017–2019 and 2023–2024? Could NCRB confirm the territorial definition of the Delhi police-district units used in 2020–2022?

Yours faithfully,
