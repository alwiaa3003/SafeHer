# Convicted-Offender Research: Privacy and Legal Review

**Status:** Research only. First recorded 10 November 2026 and extended the same day with Gazette texts and court-hosted judgments.

**Not legal advice.** Every conclusion here must be confirmed by a qualified Indian lawyer before SafeHer publishes anything about a named person.

**Scope:** Whether SafeHer, an independent academic website, may republish identities of people convicted in Delhi courts of crimes against women, and what it must never publish.

Source quality:
- **Primary, read:** the official Gazette text or a court-hosted copy was read in this session.
- **Primary, reproduced:** the wording comes from a government document that quotes the provision.

## 3. Victim identity

**LAW: BNS s.72** (L1, Gazette).
- **Prohibition (s.72(1)):** it is an offence to print or publish "the name or any matter which may make known the identity of any person against whom an offence under section 64 or section 65 or section 66 or section 67 or section 68 or section 69 or section 70 or section 71 is alleged or found to have been committed".
- **Penalty:** up to two years and a fine.
- **Exceptions (s.72(2)):** police investigation in good faith, the victim's written authorisation, or the next of kin's authorisation, given only to a recognised welfare institution. None applies to SafeHer.
- **Applies to SafeHer:** the words "alleged or found" mean the bar continues after conviction. A conviction does not release the victim's identity.

**LAW: BNS s.73** (L1, Gazette).
- Printing or publishing "any matter in relation to any proceeding before a Court" for a s.72 offence without the court's permission is an offence, punishable by up to two years and a fine.
- Its Explanation: "The printing or publication of the judgment of any High Court or the Supreme Court does not amount to an offence within the meaning of this section."
- **Applies to SafeHer:** the Explanation lifts only the s.73 bar for HC and SC judgments. It does not cover trial-court judgments, and it does not displace s.72 or *Nipun Saxena*.

**LAW: BNSS s.366(2)–(3)** (L2, Gazette).
- **In camera:** inquiry and trial of rape, BNS ss.64–68, 70 and 71, and POCSO ss.4, 6, 8 and 10 "shall be conducted in camera".
- **Publication ban:** "it shall not be lawful for any person to print or publish any matter in relation to any such proceedings except with the previous permission of the Court".
- **Proviso:** the ban for rape trials "may be lifted, subject to maintaining confidentiality of name and address of the parties".
- **Applies to SafeHer:** nothing from trial proceedings in these cases may be republished without the trial court's permission. Even where permission is given, the parties' names and addresses stay confidential. This is a further reason not to use trial-court material.

**LAW: *Nipun Saxena*, para 43 (SCR pp.786–787)** (L3, read).
- **Direction 1:** "No person can print or publish in print, electronic, social media, etc. the name of the victim or even in a remote manner disclose any facts which can lead to the victim being identified and which should make her identity known to the public at large."
- **Direction 2:** where the victim is dead or of unsound mind, the identity should not be disclosed even with the next of kin's authorisation, unless the Sessions Judge decides disclosure is justified.
- **Direction 3:** FIRs under IPC ss.376, 376A, 376AB, 376B, 376C, 376D, 376DA, 376DB or 376E, and POCSO FIRs, "shall not be put in the public domain".
- **Directions 5 and 6:** documents naming the victim are to be kept in sealed cover, and every authority receiving the name must keep it secret.
- **Current status (L4):** the Supreme Court's 24 Sep 2025 record shows the writ petition pending before a three-judge bench. The 2018 directions have not been shown to be stayed. Changes must be monitored.

**Consequence.** The "remote manner" test is decisive. In many cases the convicted person's name, combined with a year, a police station or a family relationship, leads straight to the victim. The sample shows this for intra-familial cases (S03), a POCSO s.5(n) relative conviction (S08) and offenders known to the family (S02).

**SAFEGUARD:** For any sexual offence, SafeHer treats the convicted person's identity as a fact that identifies the victim until a lawyer has cleared the specific case. Where the offence is intra-familial or within a household, SafeHer never publishes the identity.

## 4. Children: POCSO and the JJ Act

**LAW: POCSO s.23** (L5, reproduced).
- **s.23(1):** no report or comment on a child "without having complete and authentic information".
- **s.23(2):** "No reports in media shall disclose the identity of a child including his name, address, photograph, family details, school, neighbourhood, or any other particulars which may lead to disclosure of identity of the child", unless the Special Court permits it for reasons recorded in writing.
- **s.23(4):** imprisonment of six months to one year, a fine, or both.
- **LAWYER:** whether an academic website is "media". SafeHer should assume it is.

**LAW: POCSO s.33(7)** (L6, reproduction only). The Special Court must ensure the child's identity is not disclosed.

**LAW: POCSO s.5(n)** (L6, reproduction only).
- Penetrative sexual assault by a relative, or by someone living in the same or a shared household, is an aggravated offence.
- **Applies to SafeHer:** a s.5(n) conviction shows on its face that the offender is related to the child, so naming them identifies the child.

**LAW: JJ Act s.74** (L5, reproduced).
- **s.74(1):** no report "shall disclose the name, address or school or any other particular, which may lead to the identification of a child in conflict with law or a child in need of care and protection or a child victim or witness of a crime ... nor shall the picture of any such child be published". Only the Board or Committee may permit disclosure, in the child's best interest.
- **s.74(3):** up to six months, or a fine of up to ₹2 lakh, or both.
- **Applies to SafeHer:** cases involving a juvenile offender, victim or witness are excluded entirely.

**COURT: sampled judgments** (L16).
- **SH-CASE-03, para 41.3:** only a redacted copy is to be uploaded, the prosecutrix's identifying particulars are to stay sealed, and all authorities must ensure non-disclosure under POCSO s.33(7) and IPC s.228A.
- **Masked names:** SH-CASE-07 masks the convicted appellant, and SH-CASE-11 masks the prosecutrix as "XYZ".
- **SAFEGUARD:** never attempt to unmask anyone a court has masked.

## 5. Protected witnesses

**LAW: BNSS s.398** (L2): "Every State Government shall prepare and notify a Witness Protection Scheme for the State with a view to ensure protection of the witnesses."

**LAW: *Mahender Chawla*, Witness Protection Scheme 2018** (L7, read).
- **Binding:** para 35(iii) makes the Scheme "the `law' under Article 141/142 of the Constitution" until legislation is enacted.
- **Clause 9 (Protection of Identity):** the Competent Authority may pass an identity protection order. Once it does, "identity of such witness/his or her family members including name/parentage/occupation/address/digital footprints are fully protected".
- **Applies to SafeHer:** witness names are never recorded. A witness may be protected without the judgment saying so, so SafeHer cannot rely on a judgment to show that a witness is unprotected.

## 6. Accusation, conviction, appeal and acquittal

- **SAFEGUARD:** arrest, a charge-sheet or an FIR is not a conviction.
  - A trial conviction is not final while an appeal is possible or pending.
  - In 2 of the 12 judgments reviewed, a trial conviction was set aside for at least one person (S09, S10).
  - In two more there was no conviction at all (S11 discharge, S12 acquittal).
- **LAW: FIRs.** *Nipun Saxena* direction 3 (L3) and *Youth Bar Association* (L12, secondary) keep sexual-offence and POCSO FIRs out of the public domain. SafeHer may cite an FIR number only as printed in an HC judgment, and must never obtain, link to or reproduce an FIR.
- **LAW: Defamation, BNS s.356** (L1, Gazette).
  - **Offence:** defamation is an imputation intended to harm reputation. **Explanation 1:** an imputation about a deceased person can be defamation if it would harm their reputation and is intended to hurt their family.
  - **Exception 1:** "It is not defamation to impute anything which is true concerning any person, if it be for the public good that the imputation should be made or published."
  - **Exception 4:** "It is not defamation to publish substantially true report of the proceedings of a Court, or of the result of any such proceedings."
  - **Penalty (s.356(2)):** simple imprisonment of up to two years, a fine, both, or community service. **Subsection (3)** separately penalises printing defamatory matter.
  - **Applies to SafeHer:** these exceptions require a true and current statement of the outcome. Describing an acquitted person (S09 appellants, S10, S12) or a discharged person (S11) as an offender, or keeping a later-reversed conviction online, removes that protection.
  - **LAWYER:** how far Exceptions 1 and 4 cover a structured database, as opposed to a report of one judgment.

## 7. Privacy and the right to be forgotten

- **LAW:** Article 21 informational privacy (*Puttaswamy*, not re-read).
- **COURT: *Jorawar Singh Mundy*** (L10). The interim order directed search engines and Indian Kanoon to block or de-index an acquittal judgment.
- **COURT: *Laksh Vir Singh Yadav*** (L11).
  - **Confirmed:** pronouncement on 29 May 2026 in W.P.(C) 1021/2016 and connected petitions, including *Mundy*.
  - **Secondary reports only:** the right to be forgotten is recognised as part of Article 21, and the judgment sets out a framework for de-indexing, delinking and masking. The petitions concerned acquittals, quashed cases, settlements and private disputes.
  - **LAWYER:** whether convicted persons can use the framework, and whether SafeHer would have to honour later masking or de-indexing orders. The judgment text must be read first.
- **LAW: DPDP s.3(c)(ii)** (L9, secondary). The Act excludes personal data made public by the data principal or under a legal obligation.
  - **LAWYER:** whether court publication counts as "under a legal obligation", and whether SafeHer's restructuring is still covered. The DPDP Rules 2025 also need review.
- **SAFEGUARD:** a name-searchable, map-linked profile adds aggregation and permanence beyond the judgment itself. SafeHer applies a higher bar than legality.

| eCourts judgments portal: Hyperlinking | Covers only the portal's outbound links | **LAWYER** or written enquiry: no statement on others linking in |
| eCourts services disclaimer | "not meant for legal evidence"; no responsibility for inaccuracy or delay | Status data must be rechecked and dated |
| Supreme Court website | Reproduction with acknowledgement | Same as eCourts |

**Automated access.** The judgment and case-status searches on eCourts and the Supreme Court use CAPTCHAs, and so does the Delhi HC judgment search.

**SAFEGUARD:** no automated searching, no CAPTCHA solving and no bulk downloading. This research downloaded 12 publicly linked Delhi HC PDFs one at a time, 4 seconds apart, and keeps them outside Git (`docs/source-reports/` is ignored).

## 9. What must never be published

This is **LAW** where §3–§5 apply, and **SAFEGUARD** otherwise.

- Victim names, or any fact that could identify a victim: relationship, home, school, workplace or neighbourhood. This applies to all sexual offences and all child victims.
- Witness identities, family members' names, and the names of deceased women. S09 and S12 name the deceased; these names are not recorded anywhere in SafeHer's files.
- Any juvenile's identity.
- Any identity a court has masked.
- Residential addresses, phone numbers, social-media accounts, and family details unrelated to the case.
- Photographs, faces or biometric material of anyone.
- FIR copies in sexual-offence or POCSO cases.
- Any trial-proceeding matter in an in-camera case without the court's permission (BNSS s.366(3)).

## 11. Questions for an Indian lawyer

1. Do BNS s.72 and *Nipun Saxena* direction 1 bar naming a convicted person whenever the name, combined with other published facts, could identify the victim? What test should be applied case by case?
2. Is an academic website "media" under POCSO s.23 and JJ s.74?
3. Does the BNS s.73 Explanation cover structured extracts of HC judgments, or only the judgment itself? Does BNSS s.366(3) restrict summarising facts that HC appellate judgments recite from in-camera trials?
4. How does Copyright Act s.52(1)(q)(iv) relate to the Delhi HC's permission requirement and to the eCourts terms?
5. Can a convicted person invoke the 2026 Delhi HC right-to-be-forgotten framework against SafeHer? Must SafeHer honour later masking orders?
6. Does DPDP s.3(c)(ii) cover court-published judgments that SafeHer restructures?
7. What check of finality (Supreme Court SLP, review, remission) is enough to rely on BNS s.356 Exceptions 1 and 4?
8. What retention, correction and take-down procedures would a court expect?
9. If the three-judge bench in *Nipun Saxena* modifies the 2018 directions, how quickly must SafeHer adapt?
10. How should SafeHer handle a witness who may hold an undisclosed identity protection order under the Witness Protection Scheme?
